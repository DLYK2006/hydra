## Findings

(hand-written after review; every number must appear in the tables above or in results.csv — enforced by --verify)

### Setup
- Two machines, specs verbatim above. The client (tokenizer, embeddings, float64 head, argmax) runs
  on the Windows laptop through WSL2; all 12 transformer blocks run on the Intel MacBook worker over
  TCP, one connection per generated token, layers sent one after another.
- The link was Wi-Fi on the Windows side: `bench/raw/20261111_n12r6/net_route_windows_20261111.txt`
  (route to the Mac via the `Wi-Fi` adapter, recorded after the matrix). The Mac's interface was
  not recorded.
- RTT: median of the per-ping-run averages 58.8 ms, range 48.9-81.5 ms (RTT section above).
- Each cell (prompt length x cache on/off) has 6 timed runs of 12 decode steps; the anchor and
  warmup pairs are excluded. Percentiles are pooled over the 72 decode steps per cell.
- This batch ran in a foreground WSL terminal, not tmux: the earlier tmux batch hung at its first
  Windows call (`powershell.exe` specs) and was kept, renamed with a `_died_setup` suffix, under `bench/raw/`
  (see `bench/PLAN.md`, Run notes).

### Decode with and without the KV cache
- Cached decode is nearly flat in context: median per-run decode mean 278, 283, 303 and 329 ms per
  token at prompt 32, 128, 512 and 1012 (3.6, 3.53, 3.3 and 3.04 tokens/s).
- Uncached decode grows with context, since every step recomputes the full sequence:
  2.37e+03, 7.61e+03, 3.26e+04 and 7.47e+04 ms per token (0.422, 0.131, 0.0307 and 0.0134 tokens/s).
- Decode speedup from the cache (pooled means): 8.49x at 32, 26.8x at 128, 110x at 512, 234x at 1012.
- Where the saved time comes from: worker compute 79.5% / 80.9% / 86.2% / 88.5% and network+client
  20.5% / 19.1% / 13.8% / 11.5% at 32 / 128 / 512 / 1012. The compute share rises with context
  because uncached attention and matmuls scale with T; the network+client share is [Likely] mostly the
  full T-row activation payload per packet, which the cache shrinks to one row.
- Prefill (step 0) is the same work with or without the cache, and the medians agree within run
  noise (2.15 vs 2.1 s at 32, 7.24 vs 7.09 s at 128); at 512 and 1012 the cached side is lower
  (29.2 vs 32.4 s, 62.6 vs 74.5 s) — see Uncertain.
- With the cache on, the worker is no longer the bottleneck: at 512, worker 104 ms vs network+client
  200 ms per token (per-run medians); pooled p50 network+client is 191-195 ms at every length.

### Order/drift check
- First-vs-second medians within each cell stay close, e.g. 1012 on 326 vs 332 ms, 512 off
  3.26e+04 vs 3.26e+04 ms, 128 off 7.77e+03 vs 7.6e+03 ms. The first run was slower in 4 of the 6 untied
  cells, but every gap is a few percent; at this sample size (3 runs per side) that is not a clear
  order effect.

### Profiler (prompt 512, separate worker, after the matrix)
- Uncached: biggest hotspot `matmul_nt` at 64.8% of listed samples, then `attn_heads` 19.9%;
  network syscalls `__recvfrom` 10.6% and `__sendto` 2.45%.
- Cached: biggest hotspot `__accept` at 39.6% — the worker idle, waiting for the client's next
  per-token connection — then `__recvfrom` 30.7%. Compute is `matmul_nt` 20.4% and
  `attn_heads_cached` 6.85%. With the cache the worker spends most of its time waiting on the
  network and the client, matching the timing split above.

### What didn't work or is uncertain
- The earlier tmux batch hung at its first Windows call; the cause was not diagnosed. The same
  command ran fine from a WSL shell, and this batch avoided tmux rather than fixing it.
- Ping overstates the in-run network latency: twelve sequential round trips per cached token at
  the median ping average would exceed the measured network+client time. [Guessing] Wi-Fi power
  saving inflates sparse 1-per-second ICMP pings; back-to-back TCP traffic keeps the radio awake.
- network+client lumps together Wi-Fi transfer, the per-token TCP connection setup, protobuf
  serialization and the Python client (embedding, float64 head, argmax). This data cannot split them.
- Some uncached cells have slow outlier runs (two at 512, one at 1012): max decode mean 3.57e+04 ms at 512 and 8.56e+04 ms at 1012
  vs medians 3.26e+04 and 7.47e+04; pooled p95 (3.86e+04, 9.09e+04 ms) reflects it.
- Cached prefill at 512 and 1012 is below uncached prefill even though step 0 does the same
  compute; the worker part differs too (24.4 vs 27.2 s, 53.7 vs 65.2 s). Not explained.
- Profiler shares are over the entries `sample` lists (>= 5 samples), from one 10 s window per
  mode; `-O2` inlining can fold helpers into their callers.
- The Mac has 8 GB RAM and a 4-core i5; results are specific to this pair of machines and this link.
- `summarize.py` gained W7 and the generated speedup, RTT, Machines and Profiler sections after the
  matrix ran; `results.csv` is byte-identical to the output of the version that ran with the matrix.
